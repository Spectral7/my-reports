An Assistant for Your Telegram

A small program that reminds you who you forgot to answer, puts meetings in your calendar, and tells you to take your supplements. It needs access to your Telegram, so this page explains exactly what it will see, what it will never see, and how you switch it off.

1 The short version

Four small jobs

Nothing clever. Four things people are bad at remembering.

What it does

1. Tells you who you left hanging. Once or twice a day: "three people wrote to you and never got a reply."

2. Catches meetings you agree to in chat. If you write "let's meet tomorrow at 3", it asks whether to put that in your calendar.

3. Reminds you about meetings. The evening before, and an hour before.

4. Reminds you about supplements. At the times you choose.

That's the whole thing. It sends you a handful of short messages a day and otherwise stays quiet.

The one thing worth knowing up front

The program can read, but it can never write. It will never send a message as you, never reply to anyone, never post anything, never touch your contacts. Everything you receive from it comes from a separate bot that talks only to you.

So the worst thing a mistake in this system can do is send you a wrong reminder. It cannot embarrass you in front of anyone else, because it has no way to speak to anyone else.

3 The part that matters

How it reads your Telegram

This is the section to read slowly. Everything else is details.

First, something that surprises most people: a normal Telegram bot cannot read your chats. Bots only see messages sent directly to them. Your private conversations are invisible to them. So this is not a bot — a bot could not do this job.

Instead, a program logs in as you. It's the same thing that happens when you install Telegram on a new laptop: you approve it once, and from then on that laptop sees your chats. This program is one more "device" on your account, sitting on a server instead of a desk.

Once it's logged in, it looks at your Telegram in two completely different ways — and only one of them involves words at all.

Way one — the chat list. No words, ever.

Telegram can hand over your chat list: the same list you see when you open the app. For each chat, it says who it's with, who sent the last message, and when.

That's the entire input for the "who's waiting" reminder. The program asks three questions per chat:

  • Was the last message from them, rather than from you?
  • How long ago?
  • Have you written anything since?

It doesn't read the message. Not skimmed, not summarised — not read. To know that Aziz is waiting, you only need to know that Aziz spoke last and you haven't answered. The contents are irrelevant.

Way two — new messages. This is where words happen.

A second piece watches messages as they arrive, looking for meetings. For each new message:

  • It checks for time and date words — tomorrow, let's meet, call, what time, 15:00, ertaga, uchrashamiz, завтра, созвон.
  • No match, and the message is thrown away immediately. That's roughly 99 out of every 100 messages.
  • Only if it matches does anything at all leave the server.
The honest caveat — please read this one

Telegram hands the program the whole message. There's no way to ask Telegram for "just the timestamps." So the text of your messages does pass through the server's memory for a fraction of a second before being discarded.

What we control is that nothing reads it, nothing saves it, and nothing records it. It arrives and is thrown away, the way a phone call passes through a telephone exchange without being recorded.

So the accurate promise is "nothing is kept," not "nothing arrives." Anybody who tells you otherwise about a system like this is either confused or overselling.

The 1% that goes to an AI

When a message does mention a time, that snippet is sent to Claude — an AI service — with one narrow question: is a specific meeting being agreed here, and when? It answers with a date and time, or with nothing.

Two things about that:

  • The AI gets the words only — never who said them. Not the name, not the chat, not any history. We already know which chat it came from, so the name is attached afterwards, on our side. The words leave; your contact's identity does not.
  • Nothing is added to your calendar without you tapping "Add." Ever. You see exactly what the AI concluded, and you approve it or you don't.
4 Limits

What it can and cannot do

The second column is longer than most people expect. That's deliberate.

It can
  • See who messaged you and when, in every normal chat
  • Notice a time or date being discussed and offer to save it
  • Add and edit events in one calendar it creates for itself
  • Message you, on Telegram, and nobody else
  • Be paused, limited to certain chats, or switched off entirely by you
It cannot
  • Send any message as you. Not to anyone, not ever. It has no ability to write
  • Read your Secret Chats — those are locked to your phone and invisible to everything else
  • See your other calendars — work, personal, family. Only the one it made
  • See anything that was in your calendar before setup
  • Touch your contacts, photos, files, email, or anything outside Telegram and that one calendar
  • Make a payment, change a setting, or delete anything

Two limits are worth saying twice, because they'll show up as "why didn't it remind me?":

Where it will genuinely fall short

Meetings in your other calendars are invisible. The assistant can only see the one calendar it created. If you put an event somewhere else, you get no reminder for it. We'll set your phone up so that new events land in the right place by default — but if you deliberately file one elsewhere, the assistant won't know.

It will misread some messages. Dates in Russian and Uzbek are genuinely hard — "next Thursday", "the day after tomorrow", "at half past". Sometimes it will suggest the wrong time, and sometimes it will miss a meeting entirely. That's why it always asks before saving, and why it is a helper rather than a secretary.

5 Safety

How safe this actually is

Including the parts that aren't comfortable.

Nothing is written down

There is no database of your messages, because there is nowhere to put one. The place where the assistant stores things has no column for message text — it's not that we chose not to save messages, it's that the storage was built without the ability to hold them, and a check refuses to start the program if that ever changes.

What does get stored is deliberately boring:

StoredNot stored
Which chatWhat was said, in any chat, ever
Who sent the last messageAny part of any message
WhenPhotos, voice notes, files
Whether we already nudged youYour contacts list
Your supplement timesAnything from a Secret Chat

The point of building it this way isn't only today. It's that if that server were broken into next year, there would be nothing there to take. No archive of your conversations exists, so none can leak, be stolen, or be handed to anyone.

Your login file

When you approve the program, it creates a small file on the server. That file is the login — anyone holding it could read your Telegram as you. It's kept locked to the server's administrator account and deliberately left out of every backup, so it exists in exactly one place and never travels.

It's also the thing you can destroy at any moment — see the kill switch below.

The uncomfortable one, said plainly

This runs on Asomiddin's server, so Asomiddin technically holds the keys. He isn't going to read your messages — but "he won't" is a promise about a person, and you should know the difference between that and a technical impossibility.

What is technically enforced: no copy of your messages is ever saved anywhere, so there is no stored history for him or anyone else to look at, now or later. What is not enforced is the moment-to-moment: someone with full control of that machine could, in principle, watch messages as they pass through.

If that bothers you — and it's reasonable if it does — the fix is straightforward: the same system can run on a small server that you own and pay for, about $6 a month, with Asomiddin building it and you holding the keys. Say so and that's what we'll do.

Your kill switch

On your phone: Telegram → Settings → Devices. You'll see an entry called "Assistant" alongside your phone and any computers. Tap it, tap Terminate, and it's over — instantly, completely, and without asking anyone.

We named it clearly on purpose. You should never have to guess which entry is us, and you should never need our permission or our help to end this.

6 Setup

What we need from you

About twenty minutes, once.

1
Ten minutes together, in person or on a call

You'll approve the program by scanning a QR code with your phone — Telegram → Settings → Devices → Link Desktop Device. Same as connecting Telegram on a new computer.

If you have a Telegram password (two-step verification), you type it yourself. Don't send it to anyone, including Asomiddin — not by message, not out loud. That's not a formality; it's the rule that keeps this clean.

2
A Google account

Existing or new, your choice. The assistant creates one new calendar inside it and is given permission to touch only calendars it created itself — so it cannot see anything else in that account.

If you'd rather this be completely separate from your normal life, make a fresh Google account just for it. Perfectly reasonable.

3
Two settings on your iPhone

Add that Google account to your phone under Settings → Calendar → Accounts, then set the new calendar as your Default. That's it.

You keep using the normal Apple Calendar app exactly as you do now. The assistant's events simply appear in it, and you can edit or delete any of them like any other event.

Why the default matters: it's what makes meetings you add yourself visible to the assistant, so it can remind you about them.
4
Your supplement list

What you take and at what times. You can also just tell the bot later in chat and change it whenever — you'll never need Asomiddin to edit a list for you.

5
Any chats to leave alone optional

If there are conversations you'd rather the assistant ignored entirely — family, work, anything — name them and they're excluded from the start. No reason needed.

7 During setup

Two things that will look alarming

Both are normal. We're telling you now so they don't scare you later.

1 · Telegram will warn you about a login from Finland

You'll get a security alert saying your account was accessed from Helsinki. That's us — the server lives there. It's expected. But do check the timing matches when we set it up, and if you ever see a login from somewhere else, that's real and you should terminate it.

2 · Google will say the app isn't verified

When you grant calendar access, Google will show a red-ish screen: "Google hasn't verified this app." That's because this was built for exactly one person — you — and Google's verification process is meant for public apps with thousands of users.

You'll need to tap Advanced, then Continue. It looks like something you shouldn't do. In this case it's fine, and it happens once.

9 Before you say yes

Fair questions to ask us

You should be able to get a straight answer to all of these. If you don't, don't agree.

  • "Show me the list of what gets stored." It's short, and it's in this document. Ask to see the real thing on the server.
  • "What happens to my messages the moment they arrive?" The answer should mention that they pass through memory and are discarded — not that they never arrive.
  • "Who else has access to that server?" Today the honest answer is: Asomiddin.
  • "Can you turn on message-saving later without telling me?" Technically yes — it's his server. This is why the offer to move it onto your own server is on the table.
  • "What if I want out in three months?" Terminate the device in Telegram, remove the Google permission, done. Nothing of yours is left behind, because nothing of yours was ever kept.
