1 Start here

The first 72 hours

Everything below this is reference. This box is the only thing you have to act on right now.

Before day 1

1. Take three baseline readings with the meter — fasted glucose, ketones, uric acid, on a normal eating day before you start. Without a "before" number, the uric acid channel is useless later.

2. Weigh yourself and measure your waist at the navel, first thing in the morning. Same two numbers, once a week, forever.

3. Buy the list in §9 — salt, magnesium, psyllium, creatine, fish oil, whey isolate. Missing electrolytes is the #1 reason week one feels like illness.

4. Cook one batch of ground beef and boil ten eggs. Your documented failure mode is not carbs — it's an empty fridge at 14:00 and no decision made. Fix that before it happens.

5. Do not start on a Monday. Start on a Thursday or Friday, so the two worst days land on the weekend and not on Lower Body day.

The one thing most likely to sink this

Your master file names it plainly: under-eating on training days is your #1 limiter — May 11, June 6, June 20, July 1, every weak session traced back to skipped food. Keto suppresses appetite hard. That is normally sold as a benefit; for you it is a hazard, because it makes your existing problem worse without you noticing.

So for the first two weeks this protocol runs at maintenance calories, not a deficit. One variable at a time. The deficit starts in week 3, once eating this way is automatic.

2 The science

The 12 rules that actually changed

Both videos, merged and de-duplicated. Where they contradict each other, I say so and pick one.

1
Protein is the foundation now — not fat
≥ 1 g per pound of bodyweight = 180 g/day for you

This is the single biggest change from old keto. Ketones are not just fuel, they're signalling molecules: BHB binds receptors that regulate lipolysis, so the higher you push ketones, the more you down-regulate your own fat release. Flooding yourself with fat — and especially with MCT oil — tells the body there's no reason to touch stored fat. Eat enough fat to function, then let the deficit come from your own tissue.

Practical: fat is the filler that brings you up to your calorie number after protein is hit. Never the goal.
2
Stop fearing protein and gluconeogenesis
GNG is demand-driven, not supply-driven

Your liver does not make glucose because amino acids showed up; it makes glucose because something needs glucose. Infusion studies drove a five-fold increase in substrate uptake and GNG barely moved. Very-low-carb diets raise GNG about 14% at the high end — and that's hormonal, not protein. Over 90% of the raw material is lactate, glycerol, alanine and glutamine; roughly 40% has nothing to do with dietary protein at all. Fat drives GNG more than protein does, and nobody worries about that.

Practical: eat the 180 g. It will not "kick you out." It protects the muscle you spent 2026 rebuilding.
3
Micronutrition — whole foods, not "keto products"
Dirty keto is arguably worse than the junk it replaced

A bunless fast-food burger and a packaged keto bar are the same trade: swapping sugar for refined, oxidised oils. On keto the majority of your calories are fat, which means fat quality is no longer a detail — it's most of your diet. Ground beef is called "nature's multivitamin" for a reason. Broccoli, eggs, beef, fish, cheese. Boring works.

Local translation: this rule mostly means stop cooking in cottonseed oil (see §5).
4
The fat you eat should be mostly monounsaturated
Roughly: ~½ monounsaturated · ~⅓ saturated · the rest poly

Unsaturated fats raise ketones more efficiently than saturated fats do, and the MUFA sources are not the ones people expect: eggs, duck with the skin (~60% MUFA), pork loin (45–50%), dark-meat chicken, olive oil, avocado, macadamia. Keep saturated fat in the mix but get it from aged dairy — hard cheeses, parmesan, sharp cheddar, brynza — not only from fatty meat. There's more than one kind of saturated fat.

Lamb tail fat (курдючный жир), the traditional Uzbek cooking fat, is largely oleic acid — the same fat as olive oil. It's a legitimate green-light fat here.
5
Omega-3 becomes disproportionately important
Fatty fish 2× per week, minimum

EPA and DHA get stored in cell membranes, where long-chain omega-3s displace omega-6 and compete with arachidonic acid for the enzymes that make inflammatory signalling molecules. A relatively small amount of omega-3 changes how a large amount of omega-6 behaves. Since your total fat intake just went up, this matters more than it did last month. If supplementing, buy the triglyceride form, not ethyl ester — it's markedly more resistant to oxidation.

Cheap local answer: tinned sardines and mackerel. Two tins ≈ 4 g of omega-3 for the price of a coffee.
6
Fiber is not optional — soluble especially
~20 g soluble · 30–35 g total

Soluble fiber forms a gel in water that physically slows fat and carbohydrate absorption and binds unesterified cholesterol. Because it draws in water, a small amount creates a large mechanical effect — you don't need huge quantities the way you would with insoluble fiber. It also feeds the bacteria that make butyrate, which supports ketone formation. Low fiber is worse than either zero or high.

Psyllium husk is the workhorse. Chia, flax, avocado, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage do the rest. Ramp slowly or you'll bloat.
7
Minerals are non-negotiable
Sodium 4,000–6,000 mg · potassium 3,000–4,000 · magnesium 400

Glycogen is stored with 3–4 g of water per gram. As glycogen empties, that water leaves — and under low insulin the kidneys reabsorb less sodium, taking potassium and magnesium with it. This is the actual cause of "keto flu." People think they're bonking from low carbs; they're bonking from low sodium. A salt craving also frequently presents as a sugar craving.

For you specifically: you have a documented history of lightheadedness mid-session. Sodium before training is a safety item, not a performance tweak. See §9.
8
Ketosis ≠ keto adaptation. Eight weeks minimum.
And the number going down later is a good sign

You can be in ketosis within a day of fasting. Adaptation is the liver, muscle, kidneys, mitochondria and gut all rebuilding machinery, and that takes weeks to months: ketolytic enzymes are significantly up-regulated at the 8-week mark; mitochondria have a half-life of 1–2 weeks so a new steady state takes 2–10 weeks depending on tissue. Muscle glycogen falls to ~57% of baseline in week one with performance visibly suffering, then partially recovers by week 6 — with endurance better than baseline even while glycogen is still low.

If you judge this at week 2 on fatigue and a stalled scale, you're grading a machine that isn't built yet.
9
Don't chase ketone numbers
The meter measures production, not utilisation

Early on, ketones spike to 1–2 mmol within a day or two — that tells you ketosis started, not that your tissues can use it. As adaptation improves, blood ketones plateau or fall, because utilisation caught up with production. Chasing the number back up with MCT oil is not just pointless, it's counterproductive — see rule 1. Kidneys also get better at reabsorbing ketones over time, which is why urine strips become useless after a few weeks.

Your meter is a blood meter, so it stays honest. But the interpretation has to change as you adapt. §7 handles this.
10
Cycle it — both directions
Lean protein blocks down · planned carb touches up

Two levers, opposite directions, same purpose. Down: after a stretch of higher-fat keto, run 3–4 days of protein-sparing modified fasting — very lean protein, low fat, low carb — so that the body, having become efficient at burning dietary fat, is forced to pull from your own. Up: 30–50 g of carbs once or twice a week, and a planned refeed week every 8–12 weeks. Carbohydrate overfeeding raised plasma leptin 28% and lifted 24-hour energy expenditure; fat overfeeding did neither. Strategic flexibility doesn't undo adaptation — in most cases it supports it.

And it protects glucose tolerance. Keto-adapted cyclists showed reduced GLUT4 and impaired glucose handling — reversible, but only if you occasionally use the machinery.
11
Feed the gut deliberately
Keto is either very good or very bad for the microbiome

Diet shapes the microbiome independently of fiber, and keto swings it hard in one direction or the other depending entirely on what you eat. Fermented dairy every day is the simplest lever. In a human study, microbial diversity got worse at two weeks and then recovered to healthy-control levels within a few months — so an early gut wobble is expected, not a verdict.

You live in the best country on earth for this rule. Suzma, katyk, kefir, tvorog — daily, unsweetened, full fat.
12
Keto is a tool, not an identity
The thing that kills it is friction, not carbohydrate

The fastest way this fails is not a slice of non. It's food not being accessible, being stressed, and every meal turning into a negotiation. Default meals you don't have to think about. A kitchen stocked so the right choice is the lazy choice. And nobody around you needs to eat this way — you just need an environment where you're not defending it at every table.

This rule is why §6 exists as a fixed menu rather than a list of "allowed foods."

Why these numbers differ from the videos'

Protein. Video 1 says "50% of calories from protein." At 2,150 kcal that would be 270 g a day, which is not a diet — it's a protein-sparing modified fast. Video 2 gives the usable version: a minimum of 1 g per pound. So: 180 g daily as the floor, and the 50%-protein configuration becomes Mode B, run in short blocks. Both videos are satisfied; neither is misread.

Fat. Video 1 gives roughly 20% saturated · 20% mono · 10–15% poly — but those are percentages of total calories, and they sum to about 52% when fat on this plan is 63% of calories. His own numbers don't close, so they can't be copied straight across. I've kept the shape he was pointing at — monounsaturated as the largest share, saturated substantially from aged dairy, omega-6 not added on purpose — and rebuilt the grams so they actually add to your 150 g.

4 The plan

Twelve weeks, phase by phase

What you do, and — just as important — what you should expect to feel, so you don't misread normal adaptation as failure.

Days −3 to 0 · Prep
Baseline & stock

Three meter readings on a normal diet. Weight + waist. Shopping done, beef batch-cooked, ten eggs boiled. Start Thursday. If your current 8-week gym cycle is near a retest week, do the retest first — you will not get a clean max reading again until week 8 of this.

Days 1–4 · Induction
The hard part. Eat at maintenance.

Net carbs under 20 g. Muscle glycogen falls sharply and so does performance — this is documented and expected, not a sign anything is wrong. Electrolytes from hour one, not when you feel bad. Ketones will hit 1–2 mmol within 48 hours. Scale drops 2–4 kg — that is water, not fat. Every gram of glycogen took 3–4 g of water with it.

Weeks 1–2
Volume down 20–25%, no PR attempts

Drop the last working set of every exercise; keep the loads. Uric acid rises now — expected, ketones and urate compete for the same kidney transporter. Fiber ramps from 5 g psyllium to 10 g. Half-dose the Pre JYM. Still eating at maintenance.

Weeks 3–4
Deficit starts · full volume returns

Drop to 2,150 kcal (fat 180 g → 150 g; protein unchanged). Restore all working sets. If Lower Body is still more than 10% down, add the intra-workout honey — Tuesday only, 30 g sipped across the session. Uric acid should be heading back toward baseline by now.

Weeks 5–6
Add the weekly carb touch · start pushing again

One Mode C day per week — Friday, wrapped around the evening gathering. Endurance and work capacity should now be noticeably better than week 2 even though glycogen is still below baseline. Begin attempting load progression again, back and legs first.

Weeks 7–8
The checkpoint

Ketolytic enzymes are significantly up-regulated at 8 weeks — this is the earliest honest point to judge the whole thing. Retest your key lifts: leg press, leg extension, lat pulldown, chest-supported row, machine chest press. Expect legs at or near baseline, pressing possibly a little under, conditioning better.

Weeks 9–11
Cycling begins

Now — and not before — introduce Mode B. Saturday and Sunday become lean days; Monday–Friday stays Mode A; Friday carries the carb touch. This is the fat-loss engine described in rule 10, and it only works once you're adapted enough to tolerate it.

Week 12
Planned refeed · then decide

5–7 days of structured carbohydrate at maintenance — real food, 150–200 g carbs/day, protein unchanged. Leptin and energy expenditure come back up, glucose tolerance gets exercised, and you find out whether you want another block. Stepping out of ketosis on purpose does not erase what you built.

Expect this, and don't panic

Week 1–2: fatigue, weaker lifts, headache, worse sleep, salt cravings, higher uric acid, a big whoosh on the scale. Every one of these is on the expected list.

Week 2: gut diversity measurably dips before it improves. Bloating from the fiber ramp is a dose problem, not a keto problem — slow the ramp.

Weeks 4–6: uric acid should return toward your baseline. If it hasn't by week 6, that's a real flag — see §13.

Week 6+: ketone readings falling while energy is good = adaptation working. Do not "fix" it.

The most important local rule in this whole document

Cottonseed oil is the default cooking fat in Uzbek kitchens — plov, fried eggs, everything. It is a seed oil, and on a diet where fat is 60%+ of your calories, cooking in it undoes most of rule 3 and rule 4 at once. Replace it with butter, ghee, olive oil, or lamb tail fat. If someone else is cooking for you, this is the one thing worth asking about.

Two local adaptations worth knowing

A2 dairy

Video 1 recommends A2 cow dairy. True A2-branded milk is essentially unavailable here. But goat and sheep milk are naturally A2-type — so goat cheese, brynza and sheep-milk products are the practical local route to the same thing. If cow dairy bothers your digestion, that's the swap to try before concluding you can't do dairy.

The fermented dairy advantage

Video 1's gut section ends with a paid probiotic recommendation. You don't need it. Suzma, katyk and kefir are the same intervention, made locally, cheaper, and eaten daily instead of swallowed. One serving a day, unsweetened, full fat. Add a commercial probiotic later only if the gut is still unhappy at week 6.

7 The meter

Reading the Keto-Mojo properly

Three channels — glucose, ketones, uric acid. Most people use one and misread it. Here's what each is actually for.

GKI — the number that matters more than ketones alone

The Glucose–Ketone Index puts your two moving numbers into one ratio, which is far more informative than a ketone reading on its own. Both values must be in mmol/L:

The formula

GKI = glucose (mmol/L) ÷ ketones (mmol/L)

If your meter is set to mg/dL, divide glucose by 18 first. Example: glucose 86 mg/dL ÷ 18 = 4.8; ketones 1.6 → GKI = 3.0.

The interpretation flips at week 6

Weeks 1–6: a rising ketone number means the machinery is switching on. Good.

Week 6 onward: a falling ketone number — say 1.8 drifting down to 0.8 — while glucose stays low, energy is good and lifts are holding, means your tissues got better at burning ketones. Utilisation caught up with production. That is the goal, not a problem. Do not push it back up with MCT oil; per rule 1 that actively slows fat release.

The signal that something is actually wrong is not a low ketone number. It's a low ketone number together with rising glucose, bad energy and stalled lifts.

On keto, ketones and urate compete for the same kidney transporter, so uric acid rises in the first 1–2 weeks and returns to baseline by weeks 4–6. That curve is normal. What matters is whether yours follows it.

Make the readings comparable or don't bother

8 Training

Your split, modified

You keep all five sessions. What changes is volume, expectations, and the salt you drink before walking in.

Non-negotiables that do not change

The shoulder-prep circuit stays exactly as written — face pulls, arm circles, wall slides, external rotations, scapular push-ups, pendulums. It matters more now: lower glycogen and less body water means stiffer connective tissue. Still no incline barbell bench, no dips, no butterfly.

Back work is a medical priority, not a preference — your therapist's directive on holding the pelvic alignment. So when volume gets cut, Upper Pull is the last thing to lose a set. Cut push and accessory work first.

Weeks 1–2 — protect, don't push

  • Cut the last working set of every exercise. Keep the load, lose the volume. That's roughly a 20–25% cut, which matches the glycogen drop.
  • No PR attempts. None. If leg press 100×10×3 becomes 100×7, that is water and glycogen, not lost muscle. The research is explicit: performance suffers in week 1 and recovers by week 6.
  • Tuesday Lower Body is the highest-risk session — 90 minutes, 660 kcal, high-rep work at your heaviest loads. In week 1, drop one exercise entirely (calves or lunges) rather than grinding through all four.
  • 1,000 mg sodium 30 minutes before every session, in water. Given your history of lightheadedness mid-session, this is a safety measure.
  • Half-dose the Pre JYM. 300 mg of caffeine on a sodium-depleted body is how you get palpitations and then blame keto. Half serving, weeks 1–2, always with the electrolyte drink.

Weeks 3–6 — rebuild, then push

  • Restore full volume in week 3. Add sets back before adding load.
  • The honey window. If Lower Body is still more than 10% below baseline at week 3, use the intra-workout carb: 30 g of honey (≈2 tbsp) sipped across the session, Tuesday only. Muscle takes up glucose during contraction without needing insulin, so this restores some glycogen and power without meaningfully disturbing the diet. Not before the session, not after — during.
  • Good phase for your July 4 finding. You diagnosed that at 45–50 kg your hands and forearms take over from your lats. Loads are down anyway right now — so this is the ideal window to run rows and pulldowns lighter and slower, drill the back activation properly, and add straps to remove grip as the limiter.
  • Keep hyperextensions in. Bodyweight, every pull day. Cheapest possible insurance on the pelvis directive.

Weeks 7–8 — retest

Five lifts, same conditions as your master file: machine chest press, lat pulldown, chest-supported row, leg press, leg extension. Fed, mid-morning or evening, full warm-up. Write the numbers next to your June–July benchmarks. That comparison is the honest verdict on whether keto works for you — not the scale, not the meter.

9 Minerals & fiber

The two things that break people

Almost everything filed under "keto flu" is electrolytes. Almost everything filed under "keto wrecked my digestion" is fiber done wrong.

Daily mineral targets

The Tashkent electrolyte drink · per 500 ml
  • ½ tsp fine salt — ~1,150 mg sodium
  • ¼ tsp potassium chloride salt substitute (соль с пониженным содержанием натрия) — ~350 mg potassium
  • Juice of ½ lemon + stevia to make it drinkable
  • Magnesium taken separately — 200 mg elemental, twice a day

2–3 servings a day. One on waking, one 30 minutes before training, one late afternoon. This is the local replacement for the electrolyte product advertised in video 2 — same three minerals, same ratios, no shipping.

Two cautions on the drink

Регидрон is not a substitute. It's in every pharmacy here and it works — but it contains roughly 10 g of glucose per sachet, which is half your daily carb budget. Emergencies only.

Potassium chloride has a real ceiling. A quarter teaspoon a day alongside food potassium is fine for a healthy person. If you ever go on blood-pressure medication (particularly ACE inhibitors or potassium-sparing diuretics) or have any kidney issue, drop the KCl and get potassium from food only.

The moves that make it easy
  • Eat 50–60 g of protein before you go. You are not negotiating with plov on an empty stomach.
  • Take the meat off the plov rather than refusing the dish. Nobody is offended.
  • Hold a cup of tea. An empty hand invites feeding.
  • Don't announce the diet. "I ate before" ends the conversation; "I'm on keto" starts a two-hour one.
  • If you eat the plov — that was Mode C. Log it, move on, resume at the next meal. Never compensate by skipping meals the next day.
The reframe that makes this sustainable

Video 2 spends a full rule on this and it's the most useful thing in either video: keto is a tool, not an identity. You're not defending a position. You're running a metabolic protocol for twelve weeks, with a carb touch built into Friday on purpose. A social meal that lands on a Friday is on the plan. One that lands on a Tuesday costs you about a day of adaptation and nothing else.

The failure isn't the plov. The failure is deciding you've "broken it" and eating badly for four days.

  • Any restaurant: grilled meat + salad, no bread. That's it. That's the whole strategy.
  • Salt sachets in your bag. Travel days are dehydration days.
  • Whey isolate in a shaker — the rule from your master file survives the diet change intact: any time a meal would be skipped, drink one instead.

13 Honest notes

Red flags, and where I don't fully agree with the videos

Stop and see a doctor if

Not "monitor" — actually go
  • Uric acid still above 9 mg/dL (535 µmol/L) past week 6, or any sudden hot, swollen, painful joint — big toe, ankle, knee. Given your pelvis and neck history, do not let a gout flare get misfiled as a training injury.
  • Palpitations, persistent dizziness or fainting that a full day of proper sodium doesn't resolve.
  • Ketones above 5 mmol/L together with high glucose plus nausea, vomiting or rapid breathing.
  • Strength still more than 20% below baseline at week 8. That's no longer adaptation; something in the plan or in you needs looking at.
  • Any prescription medication — particularly for blood pressure or blood sugar. Tell your doctor you're doing this before you start; doses often need adjusting on keto.
