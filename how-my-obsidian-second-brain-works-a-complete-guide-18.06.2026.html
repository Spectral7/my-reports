How My Second Brain Works

A complete guide to the "My Mind" Obsidian vault — 18.06.2026

My Mind is no longer just a folder of notes. It's an active second brain: I capture thoughts from my phone, and Claude files, links, and answers from them on command. This is the manual — what each part does, and exactly how to use it day to day.

In this guide
1. The big picture 2. The folder map (PARA) 3. Capturing thoughts 4. The 6 commands 5. My weekly rhythm 6. The rules that keep it clean 7. One-page cheat sheet

1. The big picture

Think of it as a loop with three moves: capture anything into one in-tray, file it into the right place with links, then retrieve it whenever I need it. I never have to remember where something lives — I just throw it in, and ask for it later.

📱 Telegram → 📥 Inbox →  /ingest  → filed & linked note →  /ask  answers from it

Two files sit at the root and run the whole thing:

  • hot.md — the running context cache. Claude reads it first every session so it knows who I am and where we left off.
  • index.md — the master catalog of every note. The "table of contents" for the whole brain.

2. The folder map (PARA)

Everything lives in one of these. PARA = Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive — plus an Inbox and Notes.

FolderWhat goes here
Inbox/Raw captures from the Telegram bot. The in-tray. The goal is always to empty it.
Areas/One "Map note" (MOC) per ongoing life-area, linking everything in it. Current areas: Personal AI OS · Cloud Sport · AI Education · Personal.
Notes/Atomic notes — one idea each. Sub-folders: Concepts/ (ideas & tools), People/ (people & orgs), Q&A/ (good answers worth keeping).
Projects/Active projects with an end goal — one note each.
Resources/Reference material, reading digests, how-tos.
Archive/Finished or inactive. Nothing is deleted — Git is the backup.
Daily Notes/ · Templates/ · Reports/The daily journal, note templates, and research-bot output (like this report).
Areas vs. Projects — an Area is forever (e.g. "Cloud Sport", "Personal AI OS"); a Project has a finish line. When a project ends, it moves to Archive; the Area lives on.

3. Capturing thoughts

Capture happens from my phone via My Mind Bot on Telegram. I never open the laptop just to jot something down.

Send this to the botWhat happens
A plain thought or ideaSaved to Inbox/ as a capture, to be filed later.
A link (optionally + my own notes)Saved to my reading list as a "read later" item.
A link + /report (+ optional instructions)Claude reads the article and writes a full HTML report like this one, published to my reports site.
A PDF (with a caption)Turned into a full report too.

Reports only fire when /report is in the message (or a file is sent). Captures and reading-saves are free; reports cost a few cents each.

4. The 6 commands

These are Claude Code slash commands — I type them in a Claude session opened in the vault. This is where filing and retrieval happen.

/ingest — the Librarian

Processes everything in Inbox/ into clean, filed, linked notes. It reads each capture, decides what it is (idea / task / reading / reference), writes it into the right folder, adds [[links]] to related notes and its Area map, pulls out important people and concepts as little stub pages, updates index.md, and empties the Inbox. Also runs when I just say "process my inbox" or "разбери inbox".

/ask <question> — retrieval

Answers a question using only my own notes (never the open web), with links to the sources. If it's a good answer worth keeping, it offers to file it into Notes/Q&A/ so my knowledge compounds over time.

/research <topic or URL> — the researcher

Focused web research (or summarize a link) → a filed digest in Resources/, linked and indexed. This is the opposite of /ask: it goes outward to the web.

/lint — the health check

Scans the whole vault for problems: orphan notes (nothing links to them), dead links, gaps, and stale notes — then offers safe fixes. Run it every week or two.

/save [title] — keep this conversation

Turns the current Claude conversation into a durable, filed note — written so it "reads cold" later, auto-linked to related notes.

/think <problem> — reason it out

Works through a decision step by step (framing → options → trade-offs → recommendation → next step), and can save the reasoning.

The simplest mental model:
  • Get things in → Telegram bot, then /ingest
  • Get answers out → /ask (from me) or /research (from the web)
  • Keep it healthy → /lint, and /save / /think as needed

5. My weekly rhythm

  1. Every day (2 min): capture freely to the Telegram bot. Don't file, don't organize — just dump it in the Inbox.
  2. Every few days (10 min): open Claude in the vault and run /ingest ("process my inbox"). Watch the Inbox go to zero.
  3. Anytime I need something: /ask "what do I know about X?" — and file the good answers it offers.
  4. When I want to go deeper on a topic: /research it → a digest lands in Resources.
  5. Every week or two (5 min): run /lint and accept the safe fixes.

That's the whole habit. The system rewards capturing without friction and filing in batches — not perfect organization in the moment.

6. The rules that keep it clean

  • One idea per note. Atomic notes with clear, human, searchable titles.
  • Everything links. Every note connects to at least its Area map note, via [[wikilinks]]. Links are what turn notes into a brain.
  • Notes mature over time. Knowledge notes carry a status: seed developing mature evergreen. Tasks use open / done.
  • Conflicts are signal, not noise. If new info disagrees with an old note, Claude never silently overwrites — it flags a contradiction on both notes and lets me decide.
  • Nothing is ever lost. Git auto-commits the vault, so deletions are recoverable. Archive instead of delete.
  • Every filing action is logged to log.md, so there's always a paper trail of what the Librarian did.
