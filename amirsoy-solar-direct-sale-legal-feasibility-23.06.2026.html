Powering Amirsoy with Solar

Can a new company own a 2 MW solar plant on the resort and sell that electricity directly to the ski resort — while the resort still buys the rest from the national grid? And is that legal under Uzbekistan’s 2026 energy rules?

The structure you described — a separate firm that owns the plant and sells to the resort under a direct, long-term contract — is the right shape and is expressly allowed for renewable generators. But a 2 MW grid-connected plant stays fully inside the regulated system, the state “single buyer” sits in the path, and the market is mid-reform. The direction of travel is toward more freedom to do exactly this.

Why it is legal

AThe one provision that makes it work

The legal anchor is Presidential Decree ПП-57 of 16 February 2023, Point 10. It says a business that generates electricity from renewables may sell it to:

ПП-57 · Point 10 (paraphrased from lex.uz)

…“other consumers at mutually agreed prices, on the basis of concluded direct contracts, including long-term ones” — and may build the installation on property belonging to others under a lease.

That is almost a word-for-word description of your plan: a separate firm, a direct long-term contract with the resort, and a plant built on the resort’s leased land. No capacity ceiling is stated in that paragraph. The renewable-energy law (ЗРУ-539, 2019) reinforces it — renewable producers may build local networks and contract to sell power to legal entities.

Correction to an earlier assumption

ПП-57 is dated 16 Feb 2023 (not Jan 2022). An initial read missed Point 10 behind a paywall and wrongly concluded the decree had no direct-sale rule. Deep verification against the primary lex.uz text — corroborated by three independent legal sources — confirms Point 10 grants the right. The other two decrees you flagged (УП-220, ПКМ-247) describe a different model and do not authorise this plan.

Why it is not easy

BThree frictions to navigate

1

You can’t “hide” from regulation by being small

The new electricity law (ЗРУ-939) only frees a plant from the regulated regime if it meets all three conditions at once. Our plan fails two of them — see the diagram below.

2

The state “single buyer” sits in the path

Since 1 July 2024, Uzenergosotish JSC is the central buyer/seller of electricity. ПП-57’s direct-sale right is a carve-out around this — but no source cleanly spells out the exact step-by-step procedure to use it for a grid-connected 2 MW plant. This is the single most important thing to confirm with a lawyer.

3

The rules are changing right now

Uzbekistan is mid-way through a 2023–2030 liberalisation roadmap. A May-2025 decree began allowing licensed power trading; a competitive market opens in stages from 2026; the single-buyer monopoly is slated to dissolve around July 2026. Check the live rules at build time — the trend favours your plan.

The three decrees you asked about

CWhich decree actually governs this

Only one of the three fits the plan. The other two are about a smaller, different model — selling surplus back to the grid, not selling to the resort.

ПП-57 Presidential decree · 16 Feb 2023 ✓ This is our anchor
“On measures to accelerate the introduction of renewable energy sources…”

An annual renewables programme — but its Point 10 carries the standing right to sell renewable power directly to other consumers under direct/long-term contracts at agreed prices, and to build on leased land.

lex.uz/uz/docs/6385720

УП-220 Presidential decree · 9 Sep 2022 ⚠ Doesn’t fit
“…development of low-capacity renewable energy sources”

The small self-consumption regime: solar/wind/biogas up to ~1 MW, “for own needs,” with surplus sold back to the grid. A 2 MW plant is outside its scope, and it isn’t a third-party-sale mechanism.

lex.uz/uz/docs/6189043

ПКМ-247 Cabinet of Ministers · 14 Jun 2023 ⚠ Wrong mechanism
“On the procedure for selling surplus electricity produced… using RES”

The how-to implementing УП-220: sell surplus (≤1 MW own-use plants) to Regional Electric Networks at 80% of the Group-II tariff. It routes power to the state grid operator, not to a private buyer like the resort.

lex.uz/ru/docs/6502113

The landscape

Single buyer

Central buyer/seller of power since 1 Jul 2024. The body the direct-sale right must be reconciled with.

What to do next

FFour questions for a local energy lawyer

Take these to a Tashkent energy practice (Kosta Legal, Centil, Dentons, Azizov & Partners, Unicase) before committing capital:

  1. The procedure. Exactly how does a 2 MW grid-connected renewable plant exercise the ПП-57 Point-10 direct-PPA right while the Uzenergosotish single-buyer regime is in force — and what licence/registration is required after the May-2025 trade-liberalisation decree?
  2. The wires. Under the grid-connection rules (Cabinet Reg. No. 610), who pays for connection, protection and metering when the plant feeds the resort by direct line while the resort also draws from the grid — and is a “direct line” legally distinct from a “local network”?
  3. The price. Any transfer-pricing or anti-monopoly limits on the “mutually agreed” price between the firm and the affiliated resort — versus the resort simply self-generating?
  4. The money. Do the tax/customs incentives (10-yr property/land-tax exemption, green-certificate CIT exemption, equipment customs relief) all apply at 2 MW through this corporate vehicle — and what is the buy-back tariff for any surplus fed to the grid?
Recommendation

The highest-value next step is a short paid consult on question 1. That single answer — how to exercise the ПП-57 direct-sale right under today’s single-buyer rules — decides whether this is a clean go or needs restructuring. Everything else (panel choice, sizing, financing) follows from it.
